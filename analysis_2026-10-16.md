# Progress and Results Report: LLM Cold-Start Reranking on ADA

*Status as of 16 Oct 2026. Covers the work from 13 to 15 Oct: the code fix, the ADA setup, the data pipeline, the smoke tests, the prompt experiments, and the first 88 of 362 LLM runs.*

All numbers below were recomputed from the raw run outputs, which were pulled from both ADA accounts and merged (§10 lists the files).

---

## 1. Summary

1. **The pipeline is restored and reproduces the documented data exactly.** The missing `coldrank.data` package was rewritten. The split, SASRec and candidate-list statistics match `design.md`: 950 cold instances (150 dev / 800 test), 4,328 old instances, SASRec NDCG@10 0.064, and a date-plus-text heuristic Recall@1 of 0.324.
2. **The project's Direct-Rec prompt made every model copy positions.** With IDs `[1]…[50]` and a `FINAL: [` prefill, all 4 listwise models answered `[1] > [2] > … > [10]` in 90–100% of outputs, so the ranking was just the prompt order. Two-letter codes with no prefill cut this to 7–33%. This variant was frozen as `direct`.
3. **Only one LLM run beats chance: Llama-2-7B pointwise (yes/no). All of its gain comes from the displayed dates.** On test with random negatives it reaches Recall@1 0.072 [0.055, 0.090], against 0.029 for random. With dates hidden this falls to 0.022: **Δrecency = +0.050 (p < 0.05)** and **Δevidence = +0.002 (≈ 0)**. Swapping the gold item's date costs +0.044 (p < 0.05). This is direct evidence for the project's claim: the model's cold-start "success" is a recency shortcut, not a judgement about content.
4. **The other models are at chance and show the opposite date effect.** Mistral, Qwen and Phi-2 give an item *lower* pointwise scores once it shows a post-cutoff date (−0.06 to −0.12 log-odds). Direct-Rec is at chance (0.018–0.031) for every model and does not react to dates.
5. **The reasoning methods do not work at a 4,096-token context.** Structural Reasoning parses only 5–50% of the time for Llama and Mistral. 40–67% of its prompts overflow, and the history shrinks to 5 items. Many models also copy the worked example's answer (Llama-2-13B Base 96%, Mistral Fast 66–69%). None of the reasoning methods beats chance on dev.
6. **Coverage: 88 of 362 LLM tasks are done.** Baseline dev and test are done for 4 of 5 models; A2 swap is done for 4 models and A3 hide mostly. Llama-2-13B (except reasoning on dev) and most variants are still to run, about 86 GPU-hours, or ~1 day on 4–5 GPUs (§9).

How to read this:
- **The heuristics behave as designed.** Text similarity never reads dates (Δrecency = 0); the date-based rankers carry all their accuracy in Δrecency. The variants are built correctly.

- **Phi-2 has a significantly *negative* Δrecency.** Showing dates hurts it, consistent with §7.3.
- **Hard-negative R_hide is missing for most models**, because A3 hide with hard negatives has only run for Llama-2-7B pointwise.

### 7.3 How dates move pointwise scores (direct measurement)

The score is log p(Yes) − log p(No). In **A2 swap**, the gold item and one warm "partner" exchange displayed dates, so the gold now looks old and the partner looks new. Everything else is identical, so the score change is the causal effect of the displayed date.

| Model | Negatives | Δ score, gold (now looks old) | Δ score, partner (now looks new) | Gold median rank: baseline → swap |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Llama-2-7B** | random | **−0.077 [−0.081, −0.072]** | **+0.075 [+0.070, +0.079]** | **17 → 26** |
| **Llama-2-7B** | hard | **−0.076 [−0.080, −0.071]** | **+0.072 [+0.068, +0.076]** | **20 → 27** |
| Mistral-7B | random | −0.026 [−0.041, −0.012] | −0.083 [−0.097, −0.070] | 33.5 → 34 |
| Qwen-7B | random | +0.020 [+0.001, +0.039] | **−0.124 [−0.139, −0.110]** | 32 → 31 |
| Phi-2 | random | +0.030 [+0.020, +0.039] | −0.057 [−0.066, −0.048] | 33 → 30 |

(Hard negatives give the same pattern for Mistral, Qwen and Phi-2.)

**A3 hide: the "new-item premium",** i.e. mean score of cold candidates minus mean score of warm candidates in the same pool:

| Model | Negatives | Premium with true dates | Premium with dates hidden |
|---|---|---|---|
| Llama-2-7B | random | **+0.038** [+0.030, +0.046] | **−0.042** [−0.052, −0.034] |
| Llama-2-7B | hard | +0.167 [+0.159, +0.177] | +0.070 [+0.061, +0.079] |
| Mistral-7B | random | −0.382 | −0.416 |
| Qwen-7B | random | −0.628 | −0.581 |
| Phi-2 | random | −0.187 | −0.152 |

What these two tables show:
- **Llama-2-7B rewards a newer-looking date.** The effect is symmetric: about ±0.075 log-odds for the same item. That is small next to the score spread (sd 0.97), but it moves the gold item 9 ranks. Showing dates turns a content-based *penalty* for new items (−0.042) into a *premium* (+0.038).
- **Mistral, Qwen and Phi-2 penalise new-looking dates.** Even without dates they score cold items much lower than warm ones (−0.15 to −0.63), and an item that gains a post-2022 date loses score. A plausible explanation: their pretraining data ends before 2022, so a 2022–23 launch date looks implausible or unknown to them. This is the opposite of the recency shortcut, and it is untested. The planned known/unknown exposure check (design.md §7) would test it.
- **Calibration differs widely, which does not affect rankings.** Llama-2-7B says Yes to every candidate (P(Yes > No) = 1.00) and Mistral says No to every candidate (0.00); only the relative scores matter for ranking.

(Random chance for Recall@1 is 0.020 on dev.)

**Why reasoning fails:**
1. **Context.** 50 candidates + a worked example + instructions take ~3.5k of the 4,096 Llama/Mistral tokens (Qwen's tokenizer is more compact, so it does not overflow). The history is then cut to 5 items, and the reasoning is truncated before its FINAL line.
2. **Example copying.** The models answer with the worked example's IDs. With the original numbered IDs these copies would have *parsed silently* as rankings of positions 2, 3, 1…; the letter codes expose them.
3. **No recency-instruction effect.** Structural with vs without the recency instruction differs only within noise. Cold@1 for both stays at ~0.10–0.29, around chance (0.20).

## 8. Interpretation for the project's claims

| Claim | Evidence so far | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| A reranker can score well on cold-start items through dates alone | Date heuristics reach 0.096 (newest first) and 0.324 (10 newest + text) on random negatives; their accuracy disappears when dates are hidden or swapped | **Confirmed** (non-LLM) |
| When an LLM reranker succeeds on cold items, much of it is recency | Llama-2-7B pointwise: all of its above-chance Recall@1 is Δrecency (+0.050 \*), Δevidence ≈ 0; swap sensitivity +0.044 \*; a symmetric ±0.075 log-odds date effect | **Supported for the one model that succeeds** |
| LLM rerankers do well on cold items (the premise from Li et al.) | No other model/method beats chance; Direct-Rec and the reasoning methods are at chance | **Not reproduced** with 2023 7B/13B models at 4k context |
| Recency bias is universal across LLMs | Mistral, Qwen and Phi-2 show the *opposite* (penalise post-2022 dates) | **Contradicted**: the sign of the date effect is model-dependent |

**The story so far:**
- **The date shortcut is real and measurable.** Where an LLM "works" on cold-start items here, it works because of the shortcut. That model is Llama-2-7B pointwise.
- **The other 2023 models move the other way:** they penalise post-cutoff dates. This may be an artefact of their pretraining cutoff, which predates the dates.
- **Small listwise models cannot use 50-candidate prompts.** They copy positions or the worked example's answer, and they run out of context, so they are at chance with or without dates.

Each of these is reportable. The main open items: confirm the pattern on Llama-2-13B (pointwise and direct not yet run), and test it on A1 old-gold-with-traps, which measures the trap effect directly.

## 10. Changes to the protocol (deviations to note in the write-up)

| Change | Reason |
|---|---|
| Two-letter candidate codes instead of `[1]…[50]` for all listwise methods; Direct-Rec without prefill | Positional copying (90–100%) with numbered IDs and a prefill |
| Title-matching fallback parser | Some models write titles instead of IDs; applied identically to all methods |
| Yes/no scores via a logits processor (instead of prompt logprobs) | vLLM 0.6 bug with prefix caching; verified against HF |
| Llama-2-13B tensor-parallel over 2 GPUs | 4,096 context does not fit one 11 GB GPU |
| Mistral sliding window disabled | Required for prefix caching; exact at 4,096 |
| GPU memory utilisation 0.85 / 0.80 | OOM on 11 GB GPUs; does not change outputs (greedy) |
| Cold@1 is 0 by construction in A3 hide | Cold@1 counts items that *look* new; with no dates nothing looks new |
