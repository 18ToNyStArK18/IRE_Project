# Follow-up Report: 15-Candidate Pools and Llama-2-7B (Amazon VG, dev)

*16 Oct 2026. Continues [`analysis_2026-10-16.md`](analysis_2026-10-16.md) (the "previous report"). Read that first. This report explains what was changed after it, why, and what the new run shows. All numbers were recomputed from the raw outputs of ADA job 23791.*

---

## 1. Summary

1. **Decision after the previous report:** focus on **Llama-2-7B-Chat**, the only model that beat chance before (pointwise, test Recall@1 0.072 at K = 50), and **cut the candidate pool from 50 to 15** so that listwise and reasoning prompts fit a 7B model.
2. **New 15-candidate pools (`k15_*`)** were built for the baseline **and for every adversarial variant**, derived from the 50-candidate pools so instances stay paired. All invariants pass.
3. **The date shortcut is much stronger at K = 15.**
   - Chance is 1/15 = 0.067, but picking among the 3 newest gives ≈ 0.33.
   - "Newest-3 + text similarity" reaches **0.59** on test.
   - The variants still remove the shortcut exactly as designed.
4. **Llama-2-7B on the K = 15 baseline (dev, 150 pools, all 7 methods): only pointwise ranks above chance.**
   - **Pointwise:** Recall@1 **0.167** [0.107, 0.227] with random negatives (chance 0.067, p = 2×10⁻⁵) and **0.113** with hard negatives (p = 0.03).
   - **Its gold-item selection uses content.** Among the 3 new candidates it ranks the gold first 49% of the time (chance 33%, p = 6×10⁻⁵, random negatives).
   - **It also prefers new items:** a new item is top-1 in 33% / 47% of pools (chance 20%).
   - **It stays far below the date-only rankers:** newest-first 0.347, newest-3 + text 0.580.
5. **Direct-Rec and all 5 reasoning methods are at chance** (0.013–0.067).
   - Direct-Rec parses (89%), but 21–24% of its outputs still copy positions 1-2-3.
   - The reasoning methods produce a usable ranking in only **25–48%** of outputs. Failures: copying the `[code]` placeholder, copying the worked example's codes, no ranking line, running out of tokens.
6. **Verdict:** K = 15 makes the prompts fit but does **not** make listwise or reasoning work for Llama-2-7B. **Pointwise is the one configuration worth continuing.** Next: run Llama-2-7B pointwise on the K = 15 adversarial variants (dev, ~20 min on 4 GPUs) to measure Δrecency, swap sensitivity, trap effect and flood lift (§8).

### 2.3 How the letter codes work

- **Candidates** get random **two-consonant codes** (e.g. `[KQ]`, `[XT]`), seeded by pool and shuffle.
- **Worked examples** use **vowel codes** (`[AE]`, `[IO]`, `[UA]`, `[EO]`, `[OI]`), which never label a real candidate. If a model copies the example's answer, the output fails to parse instead of being silently misread as a ranking.
- **Direct-Rec** has **no prefill**. It asks for `FINAL: [code] > [code] > …`, as in the teammates' frozen `direct_codes_free`.
- Parsing maps codes to positions; unknown codes are ignored.
- This is our re-implementation of the teammates' idea. Code assignment details may differ from their version.

| Condition | Composition at K = 15 | Dev / test pools |
|---|---|---|

| `k15_a6_allcold` | Gold + 14 of the 49 cold negatives | 150 / 799 |
| `k15_a1_oldgold` | Gold (old) + 11 best warm + 3 of the 10 traps | 144 / 967 |

### 5.2 Why listwise and reasoning fail (450 raw outputs per cell)

| Method | Usable ranking | Copies positions 1-2-3 (of parsed) | Main failure types (counts, random negatives) |
|---|---|---|---|
| direct | 0.89 | 0.21–0.24 | no ranking line 34, too long 10, `[code]` placeholder copied 5 |
| base | 0.45–0.48 | 0.36–0.40 | example codes copied 90, no ranking line 65, too long 54, placeholder 40 |
| fast | 0.30–0.31 | 0.54–0.55 | **example codes copied 152**, no ranking line 98, placeholder 51 |
| structural (both) | 0.25–0.28 | 0.33–0.37 | **no ranking line ~150**, placeholder ~100, example codes ~75 |
| ssc | 0.27–0.28 | 0.22–0.30 | **no ranking line 225**, placeholder 57–68, too long 33–45 |

- Llama-2-7B writes long per-candidate discussions and often never states the final ranking.
- It frequently reproduces the instruction's literal `[code] > [code]` template, or the worked example's answer (`[IO] > [UA] > [AE]…`). The vowel codes make such answers unparseable, as intended.
- **Even when a ranking parses, 22–55% of reasoning rankings start with positions 1-2-3,** so a parsed ranking is often still just prompt order.
- These are **instruction-following failures** of a 2023 7B chat model, not context failures: nothing overflows at K = 15.

### 5.3 Where pointwise's accuracy comes from

Pointwise scores every candidate independently (log p(Yes) − log p(No)).

| Quantity | Random negatives | Hard negatives | Chance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Recall@1 | **0.167** (p = 2×10⁻⁵) | **0.113** (p = 0.03) | 0.067 |
| Gold ranked first **among the 3 new items** | **0.493** (p = 6×10⁻⁵) | 0.280 (n.s.) | 0.333 |
| A new item is ranked first | 0.327 (p = 3×10⁻⁴) | 0.467 (p = 3×10⁻¹³) | 0.200 |
| New-item premium (mean score new − old) | +0.035 | +0.123 | 0 |
| Median gold rank | 5 / 15 | 6 / 15 | 8 |
| Candidates scored Yes > No | 100% | 100% | – |

How to read this:
- **Random negatives:** Llama-2-7B separates the gold from the other new items better than chance (49% vs 33%). That is a **content signal**, which it did not show at K = 50 (Δevidence ≈ 0 there). It also places new items at the top more often than chance (33% vs 20%).
- **Hard negatives:** the 2 cold negatives are the items most similar to the user's history. The model is drawn to them (28% gold-first among new, below chance; a new item is top-1 47% of the time). This mirrors the text-similarity heuristic (0.013), i.e. it behaves like a **similarity matcher**.
- **Calibration:** it says "Yes" to every candidate (log-odds ≈ +5). Only the relative scores matter for ranking, which is why the exact verifier needed a top-500 list.
- **What these baseline numbers cannot show:** how much of the 0.167 comes from the displayed dates. That needs the **same model on `k15_a3_hide` and `k15_a2_swap`** (§8). The new-item preference (Cold@1 above chance, positive premium) suggests dates contribute, as they did at K = 50.

| Llama-2-7B | K = 50 (previous, **test**) | K = 15 (this report, **dev**) |
|---|---|---|
| Pointwise Recall@1, random negatives | 0.072 (chance 0.020, ×3.6) | 0.167 (chance 0.067, ×2.5) |

The relative gain over chance is similar at both sizes. K = 15 removed the overflow problem but not the instruction-following problems.

## 6. Interpretation

| Question | Answer from this run |
|---|---|
| Does K = 15 make Llama-2-7B's listwise / reasoning methods work? | **No.** All at chance. Failures are now instruction-following (placeholder or example copying, no final line), not context length |
| Is pointwise still above chance at K = 15? | **Yes,** clearly (random p = 2×10⁻⁵, hard p = 0.03) |
| Does pointwise use content? | **Partly:** among new items it picks the gold more often than chance with random negatives, but it is fooled by similar-looking hard negatives |
| Does pointwise beat the date shortcut? | **No:** 0.167 vs 0.347 (newest first) and 0.580 (newest + text) |
| Is it recency-driven? | **Not yet measurable at K = 15.** It prefers new items more than chance; the A3/A2 runs will quantify it |

**Is this configuration worth continuing?**
- **Llama-2-7B pointwise at K = 15: yes.** It is cheap (3–5 min per dev task), above chance, and the variants are ready.
- **Listwise and reasoning with this model: no.** They should be reported as "fails to follow the format / rank", with the failure-type counts above.

---

## 7. Caveats

- **Dev only, 150 pools:** CIs are wide (±0.06). Test (800) is needed for the paper.
- **The K = 15 deviation from Li et al.** must be stated. It changes chance (1/15) and the date-only bar (≈ 0.33).
- **The letter-code implementation differs from the teammates'** (re-implemented). Their K = 50 results used vLLM 0.6.6 and their own code; this run used vLLM 0.8.5 and this repository. The two codebases still need to be merged.
- **"Too long" failure counts are approximate:** they are estimated from output length against the token cap.
